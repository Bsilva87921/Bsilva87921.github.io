AI Strategy Lab

How I would run an enterprise AI program at a concrete-products manufacturer: find the work worth changing, prove the value, bring people along and govern it so the company can trust it. Every score, chart and dollar figure below is calculated live from the data on this page.

Northwind Concrete (fictional) 12 plants 1,800 employees 600 AI licenses Microsoft 365 · Power Platform · Claude · ChatGPT
01 · Overview

The program at a glance

Pipeline figures come from the use cases below; adoption figures are month 12 of the illustrative rollout.

1 · DISCOVER

Start with the work

Sit with dispatchers, plant leads and AP clerks. List the manual, repetitive and error-prone tasks.

2 · PRIORITIZE

Score every idea

Rate value, feasibility, risk and effort the same way, so leaders compare ideas on facts, not volume.

3 · PILOT

Prove it small

Measure a baseline, run a 6-week pilot with a sponsor, and keep a human in the loop.

4 · SCALE

Bring people along

Train by role, grow an AI Champions network, and share playbooks that work.

5 · MEASURE

Report results

Track time saved, errors avoided, adoption and cost, and refresh the roadmap every quarter.

Listen

Prioritize

Prove

  • Recruit the first AI Champions

06 · Governance

Responsible AI, in plain language

Simple rules people can follow without a lawyer, set with Cybersecurity, Data & Integration and Business Applications.

DataExamplesWhere it can go
PublicProduct literature, published specs, website copyAny approved AI tool
InternalProcedures, schedules, meeting notesCompany-licensed tools only (Microsoft 365 Copilot, enterprise Claude or ChatGPT), signed in with work accounts
ConfidentialCustomer pricing, bids, contracts, HR recordsApproved tools with data protection, plus a named owner and human review before anything leaves the company
RestrictedSSNs, bank details, medical data, credentialsNever in AI tools

Every new use case answers seven questions

  • Owner and outcome. Who sponsors it, and which number should move?
  • Baseline. How long does the work take today, and how often does it go wrong?
  • Data. What data does it touch, and what is its classification?
  • Security review. Has Cybersecurity approved the tool and access?
  • Integration. Does it need ERP, dispatch or other system data from IT?
  • Human in the loop. Who checks the output, and how is accuracy tested?
  • Change plan. Who gets trained, by whom, and what gets retired?
