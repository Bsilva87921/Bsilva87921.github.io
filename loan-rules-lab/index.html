        How to use the Loan Rules Lab

        Imagine you run lending at a bank and decide the rules for who gets a loan. The lab runs 9,578 real loans through your rules in an instant, shows what would have happened, predicts risk for new applicants, and suggests what to change.

        How a decision is made

        Every application goes through the same checks, in this order. You set the rules; the lab does the rest.

        1. Breaks a decline rule?For example, a FICO score below your minimum. The loan is declined.
        2. Score just above the minimum?The loan goes to an analyst for manual review.
        3. Everything elseThe loan is approved automatically.

        The lab then compares your decisions with what really happened: which borrowers paid their loan in full and which did not.

        The controls

        Everything you can change is in the Set the rules panel. On a computer it stays beside the results while you scroll.

        Presets
        Ready-made starting points. Conservative is safer with fewer approvals, Growth approves more and accepts more risk, and Balanced sits in between. Match policy copies LendingClub's rules as closely as possible, and Risk model lets the prediction model decide.
        Loan purpose
        Look at one type of loan, such as Small business, or at all of them.
        Minimum FICO score
        Applicants below this credit score are declined.
        Manual review band
        Scores just above the minimum go to an analyst instead of being approved automatically. Slide it to Off to skip reviews.
        Debt, inquiry, delinquency and utilization limits
        Applicants above a limit are declined. Drag a slider all the way right for No limit, which switches that rule off.
        Decline any public record
        Tick the box to decline applicants with a bankruptcy, tax lien or court judgment.
        Predicted risk limit
        Declines applicants whose predicted chance of not repaying is above this level. All the way right switches it off.
        Assumptions
        Your monthly applications, minutes per review and analyst hours. They drive the staffing and monthly forecast.

        What the numbers mean

        Every rule is a trade-off: stopping more bad loans usually turns away some good borrowers too. The lab shows both sides so you can choose.

        ApprovedHigher = more business

        The share of applications you would approve, automatically or after review.

        Bad loans stoppedHigher = fewer losses

        Of the loans that were not paid back in full, the share your rules would have declined.

        Good loans declinedLower = fewer lost customers

        Of the loans that were paid back, the share your rules would have turned away.

        Match with current policyHigher = closer to today's rules

        How often your decision agrees with LendingClub's own credit policy.

        Predicted risk

        The model's estimate of the chance a loan will not be paid in full. The average applicant is 16.0%.

        Breakeven

        The unpaid rate at which a group of loans stops being worth approving: 16.7% when one unpaid loan costs the profit of 5 good ones.

        Defect

        A loan where your decision differs from current policy. High: you approve a loan the policy rejects. Medium: you decline a loan the policy accepts. Low: you send a rejected loan to review.

        AUC

        How well a score ranks risky loans above safe ones: 0.5 is a coin flip and 1.0 is perfect. The model scores 0.67; FICO alone scores 0.62.

        Common questions, answered in the lab

        Each card is a short recipe. Press Try it and the lab sets it up for you.

        How do I cut losses without losing much business?

        1. In Recommendations, choose Balance risk and growth.
        2. Press Apply on the top suggestion.
        3. Repeat two or three times. Bad loans stopped rises while Approved moves only a little.

        Would a prediction model beat fixed rules?

        1. Look at Your rules vs. the model.
        2. If the blue dot sits above the black dot, the model catches more bad loans for the same cost.
        3. Click the Risk model preset to try it.

        Is this applicant risky, and why?

        1. In Score an application, type the details or press Load a real loan.
        2. Read the predicted risk and what your rules would decide.
        3. What drives this prediction lists the main reasons.

        How close are my rules to today's policy?

        1. Check Match with current policy.
        2. The Acceptance test lists each mismatch as a defect, most serious first.
        3. Click Match policy to see the closest match: 94.4%.

        Which loans are the riskiest?

        1. Change Loan purpose, for example to Small business.
        2. Compare the headline numbers and the Insights with All purposes.

        How many analysts will we need?

        1. Open Assumptions below the sliders.
        2. Enter your monthly applications and minutes per review.
        3. Read Operational readiness and forecast.

        Tips

        • You can't break anything. Click Balanced to start over, or Undo after applying a recommendation.
        • Hover over or tap any bar or point for exact numbers. Every chart has a View as table link.
        • A slider pushed all the way right means No limit: that rule is off.
        • Rules are checked in order, and the first rule a loan breaks becomes its decline reason.
        • On a phone, the bar at the bottom keeps three key numbers in view while you change the rules.
        • If your browser blocks copying, the text is selected for you: press Ctrl+C (Cmd+C on a Mac).

        Good to know

        • These are real loans from 2007–2010. The lab shows what your rules would have done to them; future loans may behave differently.
        • The model is good at ranking risk across many applicants, not at predicting one person with certainty.
        • Recommendations assume an unpaid loan costs the profit of 5 good loans (8 for Reduce losses, 3 for Grow approvals).
        • This is an educational demonstration, not credit advice.
        Your rules stay exactly as you left them.

        About the data and methods

        Source: LendingClub loans issued 2007–2010, published on Kaggle as "Loan Data" (9,578 loans, 14 fields). Loans are identified only by row number; there are no names or contact details.

        Data checks: in Python before use: 9,578 rows and 14 fields, no missing values or duplicate rows, and value ranges reviewed for each field. The rules engine runs live in your browser, so every number is recalculated from the loan-level data each time you change a rule.

        Risk model: logistic regression (scikit-learn) with 5-fold cross-validation. Interest rate and installment are left out because a lender sets them after the credit decision. Delinquencies were tested and dropped: with FICO in the model their effect turned slightly negative, which makes no business sense, and removing them did not reduce accuracy.

        Recommendations: each one changes a single rule, moves approvals by 10 points or less, and is ranked by net value, assuming a loan that is not fully paid costs the profit of 5 good loans (8 for "Reduce losses", 3 for "Grow approvals"). These are illustrative assumptions, not LendingClub figures.

        Note: an educational demonstration of business analysis work, not credit advice. Real underwriting rules require credit, compliance, model-risk and fair-lending review.

        The model's estimated chance that a loan is not paid in full.
        breakeven
        The unpaid rate above which declining a group of loans adds value: 1 ÷ (1 + cost of an unpaid loan in good-loan profits). 16.7% when the cost is 5.

        How well a score ranks risky loans above safe ones: 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect.
